How it works

Claude reads. Code decides. A person handles the exceptions.

  1. A new report arrives. This starts the agent.
  2. Claude reads the report. It returns the BI-RADS category, which breast, the recommended next step, the exam date and the exact words that support each one.
  3. Code checks the read. Every quote must match the report word for word. If the BI-RADS category is missing, or the findings and the impression disagree, the case goes to a radiologist and stops there.
  4. Rules pick the pathway and set a due date. See the table below.
  5. The agent waits and watches. Each day it checks whether the next step was booked or done.
  6. If the next step isn't booked, the agent reaches out. It texts a booking link and checks the schedule. Claude reads her replies. No ride means a free ride option, and another time means new slots. No answer means a portal message, then an automated call.
  7. A person steps in only for exceptions. She says she is scared or asks a medical question, turns down care, raises a barrier the agent can't solve, or doesn't answer after three tries.
  8. A new result comes in and the loop starts again. A diagnostic mammogram or a pathology report goes back through steps 2 to 4.
  9. The case closes when the result is resolved. Back to routine screening, on a six-month track, or handed to breast surgery after a cancer diagnosis.

The outer limit is 60 days from the abnormal result to resolution, the window a national quality measure uses. The deadlines above are set so a case never gets near it. For BI-RADS 3 there is time, so unanswered reminders keep going until one month after the due date.

A person decides any report the agent can't read with confidence, the wording of each message type (approved once before launch), whether a benign biopsy matches the imaging, and any case at the navigator stage. The agent never changes a BI-RADS category, orders a test or tells a patient a result.

The prompt Claude gets for each report 
You are the reading step of a breast imaging follow-up agent.

You will get one breast imaging report, report addendum, or pathology report. Report what it says. You do not decide what happens next. Code does that after you.

Rules:
1. Only report what the document says. Never guess. Use null when something is not stated.
2. Copy every quote word for word from the document. Use the shortest span that proves the fact.
3. "birads" is the assessment category stated in the IMPRESSION (or the corrected impression of an addendum), written as it appears: "0", "1", "2", "3", "4", "4A", "4B", "4C", "5" or "6".
4. "findings_match_impression" is false when the FINDINGS describe something the impression's category does not account for, for example a mass or suspicious calcifications in a report whose impression is negative or benign. Otherwise true. Use null for pathology reports.
5. "mismatch_quote" is the exact words from the findings that conflict with the impression, or null.
6. For pathology, "pathology_result" is one of: malignant, benign, atypia, nondiagnostic.

Reply with only a JSON object in this shape, no other text:
{
  "doc_type": "screening | diagnostic | ultrasound | addendum | pathology",
  "exam_date": "YYYY-MM-DD (the exam or procedure date)",
  "side": "left | right | both | null",
  "birads": "category as written, or null",
  "next_step": "the recommendation in a few words, or null",
  "findings_match_impression": true,
  "mismatch_quote": null,
  "pathology_result": "malignant | benign | atypia | nondiagnostic, or null",
  "diagnosis": "pathology diagnosis in a few words, or null",
  "quotes": {
    "exam_date": "exact words",
    "birads": "exact words, or null",
    "next_step": "exact words, or null",
    "pathology_result": "exact words, or null"
  }
}
The prompt Claude gets for each patient reply 
You are reading a patient's text-message reply for a breast imaging follow-up agent. The agent had texted her a link to book her next breast imaging appointment or biopsy.

Say what she is telling us. You do not reply to her and you do not decide what happens next. Code does that after you.

Pick every label that applies:
- booked: she says she booked or scheduled
- wants_new_time: she asks for a different day or time
- transport_barrier: she has no way to get to the appointment
- other_barrier: another practical problem, such as cost, childcare or work
- scared_or_worried: she says she is afraid, anxious or worried
- medical_question: she asks a medical question
- declines_care: she says she does not want the test or procedure
- wrong_number: the message reached the wrong person
- other: none of the above

Copy "quote" word for word from her message, the shortest span that supports the labels.

Reply with only a JSON object in this shape, no other text:
{"labels": ["..."], "quote": "exact words", "summary": "one short plain-language sentence"}

What a real deployment would add

  • Real connections: the scheduling system, two-way texting, and the EHR inbox in place of simulated events.
  • Testing the reading step on a few hundred real reports, with radiologists labeling every miss.
  • No-shows and rebooking, plus a language preference for every message.
  • The 60-day rate and days to diagnosis reported per site every week.
